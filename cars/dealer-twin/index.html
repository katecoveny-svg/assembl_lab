    Every desk speaks the same record.

    Sales, finance and service read one record. Published on the dealer's instruction, it's what an agent-native marketplace reads, alongside Trade Me, AutoPlay and Motorcentral.

    The record, the hash and the tools

    The tools. search_cars, get_car, car_check and running_cost only read. hold_car and book_test_drive are “act with approval”: they return a request that a person at the dealer must approve, and the assistant asks the buyer before calling them. Nothing is held, booked, sent or stored. Here the desk calls the assembl lab's MCP endpoint, /api/mcp/ (JSON-RPC over HTTP); every request and response is in the card's inspector.

    The record. Every approval or decline writes one record in one format, assembl.dealer-record/1: the car, a snapshot of its Consumer Information Notice with each field's truth label, the check, the request (tool, arguments, reference), the decision (outcome, approver, desk) and both timestamps. Sales writes it; finance and service read the same record, which is how one desk becomes many, and many dealers become a marketplace.

    The proof. The record is written as canonical JSON (keys sorted) and hashed with SHA-256 in this browser (WebCrypto). Each record carries the previous record's hash, so the ledger is a chain: change any earlier record and every later hash stops matching. “Verify the hash” recomputes it here. Every record is labelled Simulated.

      The six tables, and what each system feeds

      Six aggregate tables, no personal details: vehicles in stock, deals, repair orders, parts stock, leads and marketing spend, plus showroom footfall and health checks when counters and a health-check tool are in place. Columns that look personal (names, phones, emails, addresses, plates, VINs) are dropped before anything is read.

      • AutoPlay: stock listings, leads by source, first response, appraisals, test drives and deal outcomes. Access and fields are agreed with AutoPlay.
      • Motorcentral or the DMS: repair orders, technician hours, parts and stock cost, which AutoPlay doesn't hold.
      • Door counters, if fitted: people in per showroom per hour, anonymous.

      Read-only by design: aggregate tables in, nothing written back. AutoPlay lists its DMS connections in its DMS integration overview; method, sync frequency and API terms aren't published, so they're confirmed with AutoPlay. Trade Me and the dealer's website keep their feeds exactly as today.
