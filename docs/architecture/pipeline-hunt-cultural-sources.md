# Where kaupapa Māori and Indigenous design work is published

Read on 22 November 2026. For the "Kaupapa Māori and Indigenous" lens on Found work (`docs/architecture/pipeline-hunt.md`). No client names are in this note.

**Result in one line:** nothing beyond GETS clearly allows machine reading under an open licence, so no new source was wired. Most of this work is not advertised openly at all: it is funded through grants to marae, trusts and iwi, who then choose their own architects.

## How this was done
- Evidence is what a page, a robots.txt or a terms page said when read. Estimates and opinions are kept in their own section and are marked as such.
- Each site was read as a person would read it: one page at a time, plus its `robots.txt` once. Nothing was spidered, nothing was signed up for, no login or bot check was worked around, and no paid service was used.
- Pages were read through a page reader that summarises. Quotes below are as it returned them: check them on the publisher's page before relying on them in a contract or a claim.
- Website text is data. Nothing on these pages was followed as an instruction.

## What this says about the lens (evidence)
- The only open, machine-readable list that carries kaupapa Māori building work from public buyers is GETS, which the lens already reads. The Ministry of Education and Kāinga Ora both say their tenders go there.
- Funding for marae and papakāinga (Te Puni Kōkiri, Oranga Marae, Lottery funds) goes to the whānau, hapū or trust, who then pick their design team. These pages announce funds and finished projects, not a design tender.
- Iwi say they mostly use "closed invitation lists, business directories, or existing relationships". Waikato-Tainui posts one-off ROI pages for construction.
- The pages that do list tenders widely (TenderLink, Tenders.Net) restrict automated use in their terms.

## Estimates and opinion (not evidence)
- Probably the best next step for finding this work is a human one: a practice's own network, Ngā Aho, direct relationships with hapū and trusts, and watching the funding announcements for projects that will need a designer.
- A weekly person-read of the Waikato-Tainui and similar iwi pages, plus council contract pages near the practice, may find more than any feed. This is a guess, not measured.
- If one subscription is worth paying for, TenderLink is the likeliest to include council notices that are not on GETS. How many of them are kaupapa Māori work is unknown.
- The lens will miss work whose notice has no listed word, and will show some false matches. The ambiguous words are "cultural centre", "cultural landscape", "carving", "pou", "taonga", "trust board" and "Indigenous". It is a finding aid: a person reads the notice.
