    How this model works

    Tankers arrive at a rate that follows the hour, take the first free bay, connect, wait for their milk to pass a test, pump into the silos and leave. The dryer runs at full rate while the silos have milk, stops when they run low and for each evaporator clean. Two baggers fill 25 kg sacks; gantry palletisers stack forty to a pallet; the wrapper and a label printer finish it; forklifts put it away, and later load older, released stock into containers. Inputs:

    What the model leaves out: breakdowns, staff breaks, product changeovers, rejected milk and the cream, butter and skim streams. Heat, power and prices are on the season page.

    What is real here

    The forklifts, pallets, racking, belt conveyors, roller outfeeds, label printer, stainless IBC, QA bench, rail and machine guards and safety kit are NVIDIA SimReady 3D models (CC BY 4.0), loaded from web copies in codepawl/simready-glb of nvidia/simready-assets, simplified for the browser. The floor and wall textures come from the same set.

    The silos, evaporator, dryer tower, tankers, container trucks, palletisers, wrapper, buildings and people are simple models made for this page. The plant is fictional. Six inputs are the season model's own figures on the dairy plant twin: peak milk of 4.4 million litres a day, monthly shares from DCANZ's national 2022/23 curve, 28,000 litres a tanker, about 7.5 litres of milk a kilogram of whole milk powder, a 30 t/h dryer and about 22 t a container. Everything else is an assumption listed above, and every figure in this panel is Simulated.
