Transparent assumptions

Reactive true-fault stop: 6 hours with spare, 14 without. Planned intervention: 2 hours plus any spare delay exceeding warning lead time. False alarm: reactive 0 hours, assisted 2 hours. Missed fault: both use reactive downtime. These simple teaching formulas are not calibrated forecasts. Food-safety release, lockout, standby capacity and engineering approval require site-specific assessment.

Where SAP data could fit

Start with a read-only, customer-approved proof of value. Match the plant's equipment IDs to business records, then make each source and its freshness visible.

  • Equipment and functional locations: show what the cooling pump serves.
  • Maintenance history and open orders: explain prior faults and scheduled work.
  • Spare-parts stock: show whether a proposed response is feasible.
  • Production orders: identify which lines and customer commitments could be affected.
  • Condition signals: obtain from the approved historian/IoT source, not assume SAP holds every sensor reading.

Joule workshop concept: ask “What could this pump fault affect?” Assembl visualises the dependencies and scenario; an authorised SAP workflow could prepare an action for review. Exact APIs, Joule capabilities, entitlement and permissions must be validated with SAP and the customer.

Measure a pilot, don't promise a result

Agree a baseline for unplanned downtime, alert-to-review time, manual record lookup and completed approved actions. Record false alarms and missed faults too. Compare like-for-like production conditions before attributing any improvement.
