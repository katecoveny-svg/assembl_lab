    Matariki

    Matariki is the Māori name for this star cluster. It is also known as the Pleiades, and in Messier’s catalogue as M45. This page puts Matariki first and shows both names.

    The names of the stars

    Te Papa lists the stars of Matariki as:

    • Matariki
    • Pōhutukawa
    • Tupuānuku
    • Tupuārangi
    • Waitī
    • Waitā
    • Ururangi
    • Waipunarangi
    • Hiwa-i-te-Rangi

    Spellings as published by Te Papa Tongarewa in “The Matariki star cluster – are there seven or nine stars?”, checked 7 November 2026. Te Papa notes that “the information that you will find on this website and in other sources will differ because different iwi share different kōrero regarding Matariki.”

    Which name belongs to which star is not shown in this draft. Te Papa’s page does not match the names to individual stars, and other sources spell some names differently, so the matching and the spellings are left for the cultural advisers to confirm.

    How this is made.

    • Cluster stars. About 450 stars brighter than Gaia magnitude 15, picked from Gaia DR3 by where they are, how far away they are (parallax) and how they move across the sky (proper motion). It is a simple selection, not a published membership list, so a few field stars may be in it and some members are missed.
    • Depth. A star is placed at its own Gaia DR3 distance when its parallax is better than 2% and its astrometry is clean. Otherwise, including the brightest stars, it is drawn at the cluster distance along its own line of sight: 136.2 ± 1.2 parsecs, about 444 light-years (Melis et al. 2014). Individual distances still carry about a parsec of parallax noise, so part of the depth you see is measurement.
    • The sky behind. About 8,900 stars to magnitude 6.5 from the HYG database, at their catalogue distances. Brightness changes with your distance from each star as you fly.
    • Colour. Approximate, from each star’s colour index (B−V or Gaia BP−RP) converted to a temperature. Dust is ignored.
    • Sky from here. Rise times, twilight and positions come from Astronomy Engine for the place and NZ time you choose, at sea level with a flat horizon. Rise times include atmospheric refraction; the star field does not.
