How this model works

It is the department page's model, unchanged: a generic large city ED of about 280 visits a day. Each minute, people arrive (busier late morning to evening, and on Sundays and Mondays), two nurses triage them, the most urgent go first into resus, cubicles or minors, a doctor sees them, tests and observation follow, then they go home or wait for a ward bed. Ward beds free up mostly in the afternoon.

The triage mix (1: 0.7%, 2: 11%, 3: 42%, 4: 39.6%, 5: 6.7%) and the busier Sundays and Mondays are from the Ministry of Health's national ED data (2014/15, the latest detailed public release). The hour-by-hour arrivals, treatment times, admission rates and staffing are estimates chosen so the baseline lands near the national result. Scenario results show direction and size, not forecasts.

What is real here

The hospital beds, cubicle curtains, patient monitors, IV poles, sofas, desks, stools, crash cart, surgical lights, wheelchairs and extinguisher are NVIDIA SimReady 3D models (CC BY 4.0), loaded from web copies in codepawl/simready-glb of nvidia/simready-assets, simplified for the browser. The floor texture comes from the same set.

The building, ambulances and people are simple models made for this page; the people are plain figures, never anyone real. The department is not any real hospital. Built by assembl as an independent concept, not commissioned by, affiliated with or endorsed by Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora or any hospital.
