No public hospital in New Zealand publishes live ED waiting times. Health NZ publishes a quarterly district figure; the only live waiting figure we could find is an urgent care clinic (Pegasus 24 Hour Surgery, Christchurch). Hong Kong and several Australian states publish live hospital ED waits.

Right place, right time

About 46% of ED visits are triage 4 or 5: real problems that are usually not life-threatening. If that's you, these can be quicker. If you're unsure, call Healthline.

Emergency

111

Chest pain, trouble breathing, severe bleeding, stroke signs, serious injury. Call 111 or go straight to ED.

Not sure what to do

0800 611 116

Healthline: free health advice from registered nurses, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Urgent but not life-threatening

An urgent care clinic: sprains, cuts, fevers, infections. Some show live busyness, such as White Cross (Auckland and Northland) and Pegasus 24 Hour Surgery (Christchurch).

Minor illness

A pharmacist can help with many minor ailments without an appointment.

Ongoing or non-urgent

Your GP or practice nurse. Many practices also offer phone or video appointments.

SIMULATED A generic large city ED of about 280 visits a day, not any real hospital. Triage mix (1: 0.7%, 2: 11%, 3: 42%, 4: 39.6%, 5: 6.7%) and the busier Sundays and Mondays are from the Ministry of Health's national ED data (2014/15, the latest detailed public release). The hour-by-hour arrival curve, treatment times, admission rates and staffing are estimates chosen so the baseline lands near the national result when the page was built: 74.4% of ED patients were seen and discharged or admitted within 6 hours in January–March 2026 (Health NZ), against a 95% target.
