Who does what

Six small agents, each with a limit

Each agent has one job and a written limit on what it may do alone. None of them sends a message, moves money or changes an order. A person approves every draft.

  • Intent agentobserve

    Reads the order: what's in it, the window and any note for the driver.

  • Picking agentobserve

    Follows the pick: which aisle, what's found, what's missing.

  • Substitution agentrecommend

    Finds swaps for anything out of stock. It suggests; the customer decides.

  • Route agentact with approval

    Plans the van's drops around the windows. The dispatcher approves the run before it goes.

  • Wait agentdraft

    Keeps every ETA honest and drafts the message. A person sends it.

  • Resolution agentrecommend

    When a drop fails, offers the customer options. Nothing changes without their yes.

The journey in words

Order to doorstep, step by step

The same run as the map, as text. It follows your choices and the "make it go wrong" switch.

  1. Order placed, 10:42 am. The Example customer at household D orders 13 items for Tuesday, 4:00–5:00 pm. The Intent agent (observe) reads the order and flags butter as low. Draft to D: order confirmed.
  2. Picking, 2:05 pm. A picker walks the aisles of an illustrative store. The Picking agent (observe) follows: 12 of 13 found, the butter shelf is empty.
  3. Out of stock, 2:31 pm. The Substitution agent (recommend) suggests another 500 g butter at the same price, or two 250 g blocks for $1.10 more. Draft to D asking which. The customer approves another 500 g butter.
  4. Packed and loaded, 2:40 pm. Six orders in chilled, frozen and ambient crates, loaded last drop first. The Route agent (act with approval) plans 27.1 km and six drops; the dispatcher approves.
  5. On the road, 3:45 pm. The van leaves. The Wait agent (draft) drafts ETAs for A (3:54 pm) and B (4:08 pm).
  6. Windows kept. A and B delivered in their windows. Traffic adds 12 minutes on the way to C; the Wait agent redrafts C (4:31 pm) and D (4:44 pm), both still inside their windows.
  7. Nobody home, 4:42 pm. No answer at D. The Resolution agent (recommend) drafts two options: rebook for 7:00–8:00 pm, or a safe place. The customer rebooks. E (5:01 pm) and F (5:17 pm) get their groceries in their windows. The van is back at 5:25 pm; D's order arrives at 7:21 pm on the evening run.
  8. Proof. 6 of 6 in their window, 1 of 1 swap accepted, 1 of 1 missed delivery recovered, about 59 minutes of customer effort saved, 0 messages sent by assembl. All simulated.

What's real here

Real streets, a made-up day

The map is real: about 7 km of Auckland's eastern suburbs, every street, building footprint and stretch of coast from open data, with heights exaggerated three times. The supermarket stands on a real site of its kind, the same one the lab home pins: Supermarket twin · Example, not a model of this site. Nothing on this page models the business there.

The households are fictional. Each is a point part-way along a real local street, never a house and never a number. The van's route is the shortest drive over those streets through all six; it ignores one-way rules and turns, so treat it as illustrative.

The day is simulated: the order, the pick, the swap, the windows, the traffic and the missed delivery. The basket's prices are Stats NZ averages (latest published when the lab's feed answers). Rain in the live weather slows the van by a tenth. Every message is a draft; nothing is sent.
