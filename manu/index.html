Honeyeaters

Tūī

Prosthemadera novaeseelandiae

A dark, glossy honeyeater with two curled white throat tufts, a loud and complex song, and a whirr in its wings.

Length
About 30 cm
Weight
125 g (male), 90 g (female)

Look for the paired white tufts below the curved bill.

Where to look

Widespread and locally abundant on the North, South and Stewart Islands and their offshore islands. Scarce only in drier, mostly open country east of the Southern Alps.

About the models

What they are

Each bird has a feathered 3D body built around published measurements for the bill, wing, tail and legs. The depth of the breast, the curve of the neck and the resting pose are estimated from photographs.

They come from the manu library, which runs on Theo Tillberg’s Feather engine (MIT). The Aotearoa species and flora are by assembl.

How to read the models

  • The fitted view enlarges small birds so you can see their markings. The 5 cm ruler changes with the view.
  • The eight birds share an animation rig. Use plumage and shape to identify them here. The species notes link to footage and calls.
  • Adult dimensions vary. Rounded lengths and weights come from each linked species account.
  • The miromiro depicts a South Island male. Look for the white forehead spot and yellow-orange breast.

For teachers

Each page cites the Department of Conservation, New Zealand Birds Online, Te Ara and the 2021 New Zealand Threat Classification. Cultural notes appear only where one of those sources states them, and say which. For local names and stories, ask your local hapū and iwi.

Bird calls aren’t copied here. Each page links to the recordings on New Zealand Birds Online.
