How each page works

  1. Look

    Turn the model, view it from the front, side or back, and switch on the labels. Each number points to a feature and says what it’s for.

  2. Read

    Where it lives, what it eats, its adaptations and its conservation status. Every fact names its source, and the full list is at the foot of the page.

  3. Check

    Three questions per bird. The answers come from the cited sources, so you can always go back and see why.

About the models

What they are

Stylised procedural reconstructions. Code builds every feather as real geometry on a rigged body, then each species is shaped by eye from field-guide descriptions and photographs. They are not scans or measured specimens.

They come from the manu library, which runs on Theo Tillberg’s Feather engine (MIT). The Aotearoa species and flora are by assembl.

What’s known to be off

  • Every bird is drawn at the same size on screen.
  • All eight share one wing shape and one set of movements. The kererū, a pigeon, moves like the songbirds.
  • Body shapes, bills and plumage are art-directed, not measured.
  • The tomtit is a South Island male; the toutouwai’s long legs are only suggested.

For teachers

Each page cites the Department of Conservation, New Zealand Birds Online, Te Ara and the 2021 New Zealand Threat Classification. Cultural notes appear only where one of those sources states them, and say which. For local names and stories, ask your local hapū and iwi.

Bird calls aren’t copied here. Each page links to the recordings on New Zealand Birds Online.
