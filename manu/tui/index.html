Tūī

Tūī, or parson bird · Prosthemadera novaeseelandiae

A dark, glossy honeyeater with two curled white throat tufts, a loud and complex song, and a whirr in its wings.

Where it lives

  • Widespread and locally abundant on the North, South and Stewart Islands and their offshore islands. Scarce only in drier, mostly open country east of the Southern Alps. NZ Birds Online
  • Native forest and scrub, sometimes exotic forest, rural gardens and suburban parks. Tūī move with the seasons to follow flowering and fruiting, and may commute more than 10 km a day to a rich nectar source. NZ Birds Online

What it eats

  • Mostly nectar and honeydew, from flowers such as pūriri, kōwhai, fuchsia, flax, rātā and pōhutukawa. NZ Birds OnlineDOC
  • Large insects such as cicadas and stick insects when breeding, and medium-sized fruit in autumn. NZ Birds Online

Notable adaptations

How its body and behaviour fit the way it lives.

  • A curved bill, a low forehead and a long, brush-tipped tongue are adaptations for feeding on nectar. Te Ara
  • Tūī carry pollen on their foreheads as they feed, which makes them important pollinators of many native trees. Te AraDOC
  • Adults have a notch in the 8th primary (wing) feather. It vibrates to make the whirring sound you hear when a tūī flies. NZ Birds Online
  • By eating medium-sized fruit, tūī also spread the seeds of many plants. NZ Birds Online

Conservation status

New Zealand Threat Classification System, 2021

Not Threatened

  • Inc Increasing

Assessed for Prosthemadera novaeseelandiae novaeseelandiae (the mainland tūī). NZTCS 2021

The Chatham Island tūī, a separate subspecies, is listed as Threatened – Nationally Vulnerable. NZ Birds Online

Birds were last assessed in 2021, and before that in 2016 and 2012. Check the NZTCS database for any newer listing. NZTCS

Names and culture

Only what the sources say, in their words where it matters. To learn the names and stories of your own area, ask your local hapū and iwi.

  • The two white throat tufts are called poi. NZ Birds Online
  • Te Ara records that Māori sometimes trained tūī to talk, and that some chiefs taught their caged birds complex speeches. Te Ara

About this model

  • A stylised procedural reconstruction, not a scan or a measured specimen. Code builds every feather, shaped by eye from field-guide descriptions and photographs.
  • All eight birds are drawn at the same size on screen. In life the tūī is about 30 cm long. NZ Birds Online See all eight to scale.
  • Every model uses one shared wing shape and the same set of movements (breathing, looking round, hopping, preening). Don’t read wing shape or behaviour from the model.
  • The blue-green sheen and the lacy neck collar are art-directed from descriptions and photographs.
  • The model doesn’t show the notch in the 8th primary feather.
  • The kōwhai sprig is stylised too, and not botanically checked.
