Hihi

Stitchbird · Notiomystis cincta

One of New Zealand’s rarest birds, and the only member of its family. It survived in the wild on a single island.

Where it lives

  • The only naturally surviving population is on Te Hauturu-o-Toi / Little Barrier Island. Hihi have been reintroduced to seven other sites, including Tiritiri Mātangi, Kāpiti and Zealandia. DOC
  • Once found throughout the North Island. The last mainland sighting, before reintroductions, was in the Tararua Range in 1883. NZ Birds OnlineDOC
  • Mature native forest. Supplementary feeding is needed at all release sites. NZ Birds OnlineDOC

What it eats

  • Nectar, fruit and berries, and invertebrates. NZ Birds OnlineTe Ara
  • Tūī and bellbirds compete with hihi for food; supplementary feeding with sugar water helps. Te Ara

Notable adaptations

How its body and behaviour fit the way it lives.

  • A slender, down-curved bill, like a honeyeater’s, and a very long, brush-tipped tongue. NZ Birds OnlineTe Ara
  • Hihi nest in tree holes (and nest boxes), which makes it hard for them to escape predators. Te AraNZ Birds Online
  • Until 2006 it was classed as a honeyeater. Genetic studies placed it in its own family, closest to the New Zealand wattlebirds: kōkako, saddlebacks and huia. NZ Birds Online

Conservation status

New Zealand Threat Classification System, 2021

Threatened – Nationally Vulnerable

  • CD Conservation Dependent
  • CI Climate Impact
  • RR Range Restricted

Assessed for Notiomystis cincta. Criterion B(1): 1,000–5,000 mature individuals. NZTCS 2021

Threats include predation, habitat loss and disease. Managed populations get nectar feeders and nest boxes, and some boxes are treated against mites that can kill chicks. DOC

Birds were last assessed in 2021, and before that in 2016 and 2012. Check the NZTCS database for any newer listing. NZTCS

About this model

  • A stylised procedural reconstruction, not a scan or a measured specimen. Code builds every feather, shaped by eye from field-guide descriptions and photographs.
  • All eight birds are drawn at the same size on screen. In life the hihi is about 18 cm long. NZ Birds Online See all eight to scale.
  • Every model uses one shared wing shape and the same set of movements (breathing, looking round, hopping, preening). Don’t read wing shape or behaviour from the model.
  • Shows an adult male. Females are greyish-brown, with the white wing-bar.
  • The pōhutukawa sprig is stylised too, and not botanically checked.
