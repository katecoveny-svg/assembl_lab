Tauhou

Silvereye, or wax-eye · Zosterops lateralis

A small olive-green bird with a bright white eye-ring, which flew to New Zealand on its own in the 1800s.

Where it lives

  • Throughout New Zealand, from sea level to about 1,200 metres: towns, farmland, orchards, native and exotic forest, scrub and scrubby wetland edges. NZ Birds Online
  • Gregarious and mobile: they form flocks, especially in winter, and make sea crossings. NZ Birds Online

What it eats

  • Insects and spiders, such as aphids and caterpillars, gleaned from shrubs and trees. NZ Birds Online
  • Fruit, including berries and ripening orchard fruit, and nectar from plants such as kōwhai, fuchsia, eucalypts and bottlebrushes. NZ Birds Online

Notable adaptations

How its body and behaviour fit the way it lives.

  • A fine, tapered bill and a brush-tipped tongue suit a mixed diet of insects, fruit and nectar. DOC
  • Silvereyes eat different foods in different seasons: invertebrates, fruit and nectar. Te Ara
  • It colonised New Zealand from Australia in the 1850s. It counts as native because there is no evidence it was brought here by people. NZ Birds OnlineDOC

About this model

  • A stylised procedural reconstruction, not a scan or a measured specimen. Code builds every feather, shaped by eye from field-guide descriptions and photographs.
  • All eight birds are drawn at the same size on screen. In life the tauhou is about 12 cm long. NZ Birds Online See all eight to scale.
  • Every model uses one shared wing shape and the same set of movements (breathing, looking round, hopping, preening). Don’t read wing shape or behaviour from the model.
  • The grey saddle and cinnamon flanks are art-directed from descriptions and photographs.
  • The pōhutukawa sprig is stylised too, and not botanically checked.

Question 1 of 3 What does the Māori name tauhou mean?

Show the answer

Stranger, or new arrival. DOC: tauhou means “stranger” or more literally “new arrival”. Te Ara also gives “stranger”. DOCTe Ara

Question 2 of 3 Why is the silvereye classed as native, not introduced?

Show the answer

It arrived by itself, with no evidence people brought it. DOC: it is classed as native because there is no evidence that it was artificially introduced. DOC

Question 3 of 3 Its 2021 qualifier is SO, Secure Overseas. What does that tell you?

Show the answer

It is also secure in other countries, not just here. The qualifier means the bird is secure overseas. NZ Birds Online notes it colonised New Zealand from Australia. NZTCS 2021NZ Birds Online
