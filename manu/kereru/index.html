Kererū

New Zealand pigeon · Hemiphaga novaeseelandiae

A big, glossy fruit pigeon that carries the seeds of large-fruited trees from one patch of forest to another.

Where it lives

  • Widespread from Northland to Stewart Island, and on some offshore islands. NZ Birds Online
  • Many kinds of forest, and also farmland shelterbelts, urban parks and gardens. NZ Birds Online

What it eats

  • Buds, leaves, flowers and fruit of many plants, native and exotic. Leaves are eaten when fruit is scarce, especially kōwhai, tree lucerne, willow and poplar. NZ Birds OnlineTe Ara
  • Native fruit includes miro, tawa, pūriri, taraire, kahikatea, nīkau and coprosma. Te Ara

Notable adaptations

How its body and behaviour fit the way it lives.

  • A wide gape. Kererū and parea are now the only birds big enough to swallow large fruit, such as karaka, miro, tawa and taraire, and spread their seeds. NZ Birds OnlineDOC
  • NZ Birds Online calls the kererū the most important vector for moving seeds between widely spaced fragments of native forest. NZ Birds Online
  • Its wingbeats make a distinctive sound, and males give display flights at the start of nesting. NZ Birds Online
  • It lays a single egg. Chicks are fed a protein-rich milky secretion from the walls of the parents’ crops. NZ Birds OnlineDOC

Conservation status

Not Threatened

  • CD Conservation Dependent

Assessed for Hemiphaga novaeseelandiae. NZTCS 2021

Not Threatened with the qualifier Conservation Dependent: it relies on ongoing management. The main threat is introduced predators (feral cats, possums, stoats and ship rats), along with collisions and illegal hunting. NZTCS 2021NZ Birds Online

Birds were last assessed in 2021, and before that in 2016 and 2012. Check the NZTCS database for any newer listing. NZTCS

Names and culture

Only what the sources say, in their words where it matters. To learn the names and stories of your own area, ask your local hapū and iwi.

  • Te Ara notes that to Māori the kererū was an important source of food. Kererū have been protected from hunting since 1912. Te Ara
  • In Northland, and in parts of Canterbury, the bird is known as kūkupa. Te AraDOC

About this model

  • A stylised procedural reconstruction, not a scan or a measured specimen. Code builds every feather, shaped by eye from field-guide descriptions and photographs.
  • All eight birds are drawn at the same size on screen. In life the kererū is about 50 cm long. NZ Birds OnlineTe Ara See all eight to scale.
  • Every model uses one shared wing shape and the same set of movements (breathing, looking round, hopping, preening). Don’t read wing shape or behaviour from the model.
  • A pigeon, animated with the same songbird movements as the other seven. Its posture, hops and preening aren’t based on kererū behaviour.
  • Drawn at the same size on screen as the 12 cm tauhou: in life it is about four times as long.
  • The kōwhai sprig is stylised too, and not botanically checked.

Question 3 of 3 What is the main threat to kererū?
