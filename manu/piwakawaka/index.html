Pīwakawaka

New Zealand fantail · Rhipidura fuliginosa

A small, restless insect-catcher that twists and turns in the air, steering with a broad, fanned tail.

Where it lives

  • From Northland to Stewart Island, and on some islands of the Chatham group. NZ Birds Online
  • Native and exotic forest, shrubland, plantations, shelterbelts, orchards and well-treed parks and gardens, from sea level to the snow line. NZ Birds OnlineDOC

What it eats

  • Mainly small invertebrates such as moths, flies, beetles and spiders, and sometimes small fruit. NZ Birds Online
  • Three ways of hunting: hawking (snapping insects from swarms in open spaces), flushing insects out of dense plants, and following other animals, people included, to catch the insects they disturb. DOCTe Ara

Notable adaptations

How its body and behaviour fit the way it lives.

  • Fantails use their broad tails to change direction quickly while hunting for insects. DOC
  • When hunting in foliage they flick their wings and fan their tails, which may startle hidden prey into moving. NZ Birds Online
  • Populations can drop sharply during long spells of cold, wet weather, but usually recover within a year or two. Fantails make up for heavy losses of eggs and chicks by raising many young. NZ Birds OnlineDOC

Conservation status

New Zealand Threat Classification System, 2021

Not Threatened

  • EF Extreme Fluctuations

Assessed for Rhipidura fuliginosa placabilis (North Island) and R. f. fuliginosa (South Island). NZTCS 2021

The Chatham Island fantail is At Risk – Naturally Uncommon. DOC

Birds were last assessed in 2021, and before that in 2016 and 2012. Check the NZTCS database for any newer listing. NZTCS

Names and culture

Only what the sources say, in their words where it matters. To learn the names and stories of your own area, ask your local hapū and iwi.

  • Te Ara notes that fantails have been great favourites with Māori, playing a prominent role in many legends. Te Ara
  • DOC’s fantail page tells one, in which the fantail is linked to the presence of death in the world. Read it there, in DOC’s words. DOC

About this model

  • A stylised procedural reconstruction, not a scan or a measured specimen. Code builds every feather, shaped by eye from field-guide descriptions and photographs.
  • All eight birds are drawn at the same size on screen. In life the pīwakawaka is about 16 cm long. NZ Birds Online See all eight to scale.
  • Every model uses one shared wing shape and the same set of movements (breathing, looking round, hopping, preening). Don’t read wing shape or behaviour from the model.
  • Shows the pied form. A black form, with no white on its tail feathers, makes up to 5% of South Island birds and is rare in the North Island.
  • The model’s tail only fans a little as it moves.
  • The mānuka sprig is stylised too, and not botanically checked.

Question 1 of 3 What does a fantail use its broad tail for while hunting?

Show the answer

Changing direction quickly. DOC: fantails use their broad tails to change direction quickly while hunting for insects. DOC

Question 2 of 3 Why do fantails often follow people?

Show the answer

To catch insects that people disturb. DOC and Te Ara both describe fantails following people and other animals to catch disturbed insects, such as sandflies. DOCTe Ara

Question 3 of 3 Fantails are Not Threatened, with the qualifier Extreme Fluctuations. What can make their numbers drop sharply?

Show the answer

Long spells of cold, wet weather. NZ Birds Online: populations can drop sharply during prolonged cold, wet weather, but usually recover within a year or two. NZ Birds OnlineNZTCS 2021
