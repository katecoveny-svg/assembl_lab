Miromiro

Tomtit · Petroica macrocephala (model: Petroica macrocephala macrocephala)

A small, big-headed forest bird that watches from a branch, then pounces.

Where it lives

  • Forest and shrubland, both native and exotic, from sea level to the subalpine zone. Rarely seen in heavily modified open land such as farmland and suburbs. NZ Birds Online
  • Across the North and South Islands, Stewart Island and several offshore islands, with their own subspecies on the Chatham, Snares and Auckland Islands. NZ Birds OnlineDOC

What it eats

  • Small invertebrates: spiders, amphipods, beetles, flies, moths and their larvae, and wētā. Occasionally small fruit, swallowed whole. NZ Birds Online

Notable adaptations

How its body and behaviour fit the way it lives.

  • It hunts by perch-and-pounce: it scans from a branch or trunk, then flies out to catch prey or moves to a new perch. NZ Birds OnlineTe Ara
  • A large head and a short bill. DOC
  • Both sexes have a white spot at the base of the upper bill, which can enlarge during displays. NZ Birds Online

Conservation status

New Zealand Threat Classification System, 2021

Not Threatened

Assessed for Petroica macrocephala macrocephala (South Island) and P. m. toitoi (North Island). NZTCS 2021

The Chatham Island tomtit is Nationally Endangered; the Snares and Auckland Island tomtits are Naturally Uncommon. NZ Birds Online

Birds were last assessed in 2021, and before that in 2016 and 2012. Check the NZTCS database for any newer listing. NZTCS

Names and culture

Only what the sources say, in their words where it matters. To learn the names and stories of your own area, ask your local hapū and iwi.

  • DOC gives miromiro as the name of the North Island tomtit and ngirungiru for the South Island tomtit. NZ Birds Online lists miromiro, ngirungiru, piropiro and maui-potiki as Māori names, and Te Ara gives kōmiromiro. DOCNZ Birds OnlineTe Ara

About this model

  • A stylised procedural reconstruction, not a scan or a measured specimen. Code builds every feather, shaped by eye from field-guide descriptions and photographs.
  • All eight birds are drawn at the same size on screen. In life the miromiro is about 13 cm long. NZ Birds OnlineDOC See all eight to scale.
  • Every model uses one shared wing shape and the same set of movements (breathing, looking round, hopping, preening). Don’t read wing shape or behaviour from the model.
  • Shows a South Island male (Petroica macrocephala macrocephala), with the yellow-orange breast. A North Island male would be white below.
  • The mānuka sprig is stylised too, and not botanically checked.

Question 1 of 3 How does a tomtit usually catch its food?

Show the answer

Perch-and-pounce: it watches from a branch, then flies out to catch prey. NZ Birds Online describes perch-and-pounce foraging; Te Ara says it catches prey by scanning a wide area and pouncing. NZ Birds OnlineTe Ara

Question 2 of 3 How can you tell a South Island male tomtit from a North Island male?

Show the answer

The South Island male has a yellow-orange breast and belly. NZ Birds Online: in the South Island subspecies the white underparts are replaced by yellow-orange. NZ Birds Online

Question 3 of 3 How many tomtit subspecies are there?

Show the answer

Five. DOC: there are five subspecies, each restricted to one island or island group. DOC
