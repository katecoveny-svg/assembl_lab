    Vision detective
    A camera has data, not understanding. Measure an image’s pixels on your device. This is classical computer vision, not an AI that identifies things.
    
   
    
    
    
     
    
     
    
     
     
    Your image stays on this device. Avoid faces, names, addresses or anything private.

    Try this
    1. Predict which sample has the most edge pixels. Then measure all three.
    2. Change the sensitivity and measure again. What else gets marked?
    3. Could any of these numbers tell you a leaf’s species? Why not?
    
     
    Why would an AI vision model get it wrong?
    Models learn patterns from training examples. A new angle, unusual light, a rare species or a biased dataset can change the answer. A confident sentence is not proof: check the source, compare alternatives and ask someone with local knowledge.
    
     
    Privacy and Māori data
    Don’t upload people or private places to services you don’t know. Māori data can be taonga, and decisions about collecting and using it need the right people involved. A relationship with local whānau, hapū or iwi can’t be replaced by a model.
    
    
    
   
    
  
    

  
  
    
   
    AI judgement
    Fluent is not the same as true. Use the source card to judge three answers: what is supported, what goes too far, and what is made up.
    
   
    Illustrative
    Source card: restoring a local stream
    Students surveyed 40 metres of stream bank. They counted 18 native plants before restoration. One year later, after planting and weed removal, they counted 43. The survey did not measure water quality or fish numbers.
    An invented classroom dataset for practice, not a published study.
    
   
    
   
    
   
    
   
    A four-check habit
    1. Purpose: is the tool helping me learn, or doing my thinking for me?
    2. Evidence: which claims can I check in a source I trust?
    3. People: does this use private, cultural or copyrighted material fairly?
    4. Ownership: can I explain and redo the final work myself?
    
    
    Follow your school’s assessment and AI rules. The Ministry of Education says generative AI is not permitted in NCEA external assessment, and assessed work must be your own.
