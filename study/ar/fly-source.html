A real, bounded landscape

WGS84 bounds: west 170.03° E, east 170.25° E, north 43.53° S, south 43.69° S. Approximate local extent 17.71 × 17.79 km. This is the Aoraki destination already explored in the lesson; the other flight destinations are not included.

The 50 cm terrain width represents a horizontal scale of approximately 1:35,425. Elevation is exaggerated 1.5×, giving a vertical scale of 1:23,617. A 1 cm plinth is an added display base. The terrain height is measured upward from the lowest extracted sample, not sea level; labels retain the source elevation values.

Read the relief

Marker 1 identifies the highest sampled ridge (3,680 m); marker 2 identifies the lowest sampled valley (649 m). These are grid samples, not surveyed peak or valley elevations. Look across sharp ridges and broader glacial valleys. Ask how uplift and erosion can act together. North and the 5 km bar refer to the horizontal plane. Rose-to-white colour encodes relative elevation, not vegetation, geology or current snow cover.

Data and limitations

Retrieved 9 November 2026 from the lesson's existing LINZ elevation Terrain-RGB service: WebMercatorQuad zoom 11, columns 1991–1992, rows 1299–1300. Four tiles were cropped and bilinearly resampled at pixel centres to a 129 × 129 grid. The source pixels are approximately 55 m at this latitude; the exported grid spacing is approximately 139 m. Horizontal distances use a local equirectangular approximation centred at 43.61° S.

LINZ's merged elevation product combines available elevation sources of varying age and resolution, including contour-derived terrain where LiDAR is unavailable. This simplified cartographic display is not suitable for terrain analysis, navigation, engineering or hazard assessment. There are no buildings, trees, aerial photographs or current glacier boundaries. Native lighting is illustrative. Flight, map layers and lesson controls remain in the browser.

Physical iPhone and Android placement has not been tested. The prepared GLB and USDZ are static models; browser checks do not verify native scale, materials or camera placement.
