# Te reo Māori in assembly: research notes and sources

Last updated 25 October 2026 (practice-pack machines added: vending machine, phone, queue, floor-plan studio; NCEA steps on the downhill and vinyl machines). These notes record what guidance shaped the te reo Māori added to the study machines, where every kupu (word) came from, and what still needs review by someone with cultural standing. Nothing here was invented: each kupu was looked up and its entry opened on Te Aka Māori Dictionary, and the link is recorded below.

## 1. What the Ministry of Education expects

**The curriculum is changing, and te reo sits differently in it.**
- The national curriculum timeline on Tāhūrangi: English, Mathematics and Statistics, Te Reo Rangatira and Pāngarau (Years 0–10) are required from 2026; Years 0–8 Science and Social Sciences (and Pūtaiao, Pūmanawa Tangata) from 2027, when Years 9–10 also move to all learning areas; Year 11 in 2028, Year 12 in 2029, Year 13 in 2030. Source: [Tāhūrangi, national curriculum timelines](https://tahurangi.education.govt.nz/national-curriculum-timelines).
- Draft Years 0–10 content for Science, Social Sciences, Health, The Arts, Learning Languages and Technology, and a draft of the framework (Te Mātaiaho), were released on 28 October 2025, with feedback open until 24 April 2026. Source: [Ministry of Education news, 28 Oct 2025](https://www.education.govt.nz/news/draft-curriculum-content-released-years-0-10).
- The drafts use less te reo than earlier versions. The Ministry's curriculum lead said "we haven't used as much Te Reo as we've used in the past", that te reo and te ao Māori concepts are still used across learning areas, and that students will be taught to read Māori words correctly. Source: [ODT/RNZ, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.odt.co.nz/news/national/less-m%C4%81ori-school-curriculums-defended). NZAEE's critique of the draft framework says Te Tiriti moved from "central" to "reflecting" and the 2007 principle on "protecting and providing for te reo Māori, tikanga, mātauranga Māori" was removed: [NZAEE guidance on the draft Te Mātaiaho](https://www.nzaee.org.nz/news-and-publications/guidance-for-draft-te-mataiaho-consultation). Reports in early 2026 describe concerns from principals and Māori education leaders about this: [Waatea News, 4 Feb 2026](https://waateanews.com/2026/02/04/national-focus-on-maori-education-as-schools-start-the-year-new-curriculum-and-support-package-unveiled/).
- What this means for assembly: te reo is not decorative here. We add accurate subject vocabulary in context, which helps students read and say Māori words correctly, without claiming to teach te reo or mātauranga Māori.

**School boards still have a legal Te Tiriti objective.** Under section 127 of the Education and Training Act 2020, a board gives effect to Te Tiriti o Waitangi by, among other things, taking "all reasonable steps to make instruction available in te reo Māori and tikanga Māori" (in force from 1 January 2021). Source: [Ministry of Education, amending school board objectives](https://www.education.govt.nz/amending-school-board-objectives/).

**Te Aho Arataki Marau mō te Ako i Te Reo Māori – Kura Auraki** (curriculum guidelines for teaching and learning te reo Māori in English-medium schools). The old TKI pages now redirect to New Zealand Curriculum Online, which says the guidelines are "intended to help every English-medium school in New Zealand to design and shape a curriculum" that includes te reo, that "Māori students strengthen their connection to their identities", that te reo "is guaranteed protection under Te Tiriti o Waitangi", and that learning it helps all students "build an appreciation of cultural knowledge, language, and understandings". It notes the resources will be reviewed to align with the refreshed curriculum. Source: [NZ Curriculum Online, te reo Māori resources](https://newzealandcurriculum.tahurangi.education.govt.nz/new-zealand-curriculum-online/learning-content-resources/te-reo-m-ori/5637157326.c) (redirect target of the old [tereomaori.tki.org.nz guidelines](https://tereomaori.tki.org.nz/Curriculum-guidelines/The-importance-of-learning-te-reo-Maori)). The PDF link from TKI now returns 403, so its "written code" section could not be opened; macron guidance below comes from other sources.

**Tātaiako** (cultural competencies for teachers of Māori learners, hosted by the Teaching Council): five competencies, wānanga, whanaungatanga, manaakitanga, tangata whenuatanga and ako. Relevant here: manaakitanga is "showing genuine care, respect, and integrity towards Māori beliefs, language, and culture", and tangata whenuatanga is "creating learning environments where their identity, language, and culture are valued". Source: [Teaching Council, Tātaiako](https://teachingcouncil.nz/resource-centre/tataiako-cultural-competencies-for-teachers-of-maori-learners/).

**Ka Hikitia – Ka Hāpaitia** (Māori Education Strategy, refreshed 2020, still listed as current, page updated 20 Oct 2026). Outcome domain *Te Tuakiritanga*: identity, language and culture matter for Māori learners. It is a companion to **Tau Mai Te Reo**, the Māori language in education strategy, which covers all learners in English- and Māori-medium settings and aims for learners to value and use everyday Māori words, under the Maihi Karauna vision "Kia māhorahora te reo – everywhere, everyway, for everyone, every day". Sources: [Ka Hikitia – Ka Hāpaitia](https://www.education.govt.nz/our-work/strategies-policies-and-programmes/maori-education-and-language/ka-hikitia-ka-hapaitia), [Tau Mai Te Reo](https://education.govt.nz/our-work/overall-strategies-and-policies/tau-mai-te-reo).

**Writing te reo correctly.** Te Aka Māori Dictionary follows "the orthographic conventions of Te Taura Whiri i te Reo Māori, including the use of the macron to show vowel length"; vowel length "usually carries meaning, e.g. kēkē (armpit), kekē (to creak), keke (cake)". Source: [Te Aka, dictionary info](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/dictionary-info). Te Taura Whiri's own site returned 403 to automated requests, so its guidelines could not be opened directly.

**How we applied this (the rules for this work):**
1. Kupu in context: each term sits beside the idea it names, on the step where students meet that idea, not as decoration.
2. Accuracy over quantity: 6–12 terms per machine, all verified; where Te Aka gives two terms, or a word with a second meaning, the card says so.
3. Prefer established curriculum terms: entries that cite the Ministry's Māori-medium dictionaries *Te Reo Pūtaiao* (2009, science) and *Te Reo Pāngarau* (2010, maths) were preferred (Te Aka source codes RP and TRP, listed on [Te Aka's dictionary project page](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/dictionary-project)). No glossary for Tikanga ā-Iwi (social sciences) was found among Te Aka's sources, so civics and market terms rely on general Te Aka entries (several are loanwords).
4. Macrons always; `lang="mi"` on every te reo term so screen readers switch pronunciation.
5. No pronunciation audio. Each term links to its Te Aka entry instead.
6. Gaps are left as gaps: no term was added where Te Aka had none (see section 4).

## 2. What was built

- **kit.js** (backup: kit.before-kupu.js): new optional `kupu` config on `StudyKit.mount` (`{mi, en, ta, note, chip}`; `ta` = Te Aka word id). `step.kupu` lists terms for a step (names from the machine list, or extra objects). The current step's terms show in a "Kupu Māori" card in the side column; all of a machine's terms sit in a collapsible "Kupu Māori in this machine" list above the footer. A **Te reo labels** toggle appears in the header when any term maps to a readout chip, and adds the term in brackets (e.g. SPEED (tere)). It's saved as `studyreo` in localStorage, with try/catch. Also exported: `StudyKit.kupuItem`, `StudyKit.teAka`. All existing APIs are unchanged.
- **kit.css** (backup: kit.before-kupu.css): calm dashed card, plum and cream, mint only for links and the toggle; no yellow.
- **m/*.js** (backups in m/.before-kupu/): a `KUPU` list plus a step map, inserted just before `StudyKit.mount`, and `kupu:KUPU` passed to mount. No physics or behaviour was changed.
- **m/read.js**: a collapsed kupu list on the *My progress* screen (hidden when printing).
- **hub.html → index.html**: "Kia ora." opens the hero paragraph; one whakataukī with its translation and source sits below the hero.

## 6. Hub greeting and whakataukī

- "Kia ora." opens the hero paragraph ([Te Aka: Kia ora!](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/2608)).
- Whakataukī: *Ko te manu e kai ana i te miro, nōna te ngahere; ko te manu e kai ana i te mātauranga, nōna te ao.* — "The bird that partakes of the miro berry reigns in the forest; the bird that partakes of the power of knowledge has access to the world." Text and translation are exactly as in the Ministry of Education's *Ngā Whakataukī* classroom resource (Kaupapa: Mātauranga): [akopanuku.education.govt.nz PDF](https://akopanuku.education.govt.nz/assets/ResourceFiles/Resource_Nga-whakatauki_File.pdf). It fits the kererū and miro already used in the reading machine. The same resource also lists *Whaowhia te kete mātauranga* (Fill the basket of knowledge), a shorter alternative; Te Aka records it as *Whaowhina te kete mātauranga* ([48499](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/48499)), and both are valid passive forms of *whao* ([10098](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/10098)).

## 7. For review by someone with cultural standing

Please have a kaiako of te reo Māori, or a Māori education adviser, and ideally local mana whenua, check these before wide release:
1. **The whakataukī choice and its use on a commercial study product.** The resource gives no iwi attribution or origin; confirm it suits this use and setting.
2. **Matariki, Puanga and maramataka** (sky machine). The notes follow Te Aka: Matariki's pre-dawn rising marks the Māori new year; for some iwi Puanga does; maramataka names are similar but their order varies by iwi. Te Aka dates Matariki's rising to "about mid-June", while the reading machine's text (and the public holiday) put it in late June or early July, so the card says "in midwinter". Check that the framing and dates are right for your audience, and whether local iwi practice (e.g. Puanga in Taranaki and Whanganui) should be named.
3. **Papatūānuku as "Earth"**: the card notes she is the Earth mother as well as the name Te Reo Pūtaiao uses for the planet. Confirm this sits respectfully in a physics context.
4. **Genetics terms** (*ira*, *pītau ira*, *mātāira*) next to the DNA-profiling step: genetics touches whakapapa and Māori data sovereignty. Confirm the framing is appropriate.
5. **Words with deeper meanings shown with an everyday gloss**: *utu* (price, and reciprocity), *papatipu* (mass, and ancestral land), *kāwanatanga* (government; a word with particular significance in Te Tiriti o Waitangi), *kaitiakitanga*, *hapū*, *mana whenua* (already in the market inquiry). Confirm the short notes are adequate.
6. **Iwi-specific content**: none was added. The fictional Kōwhai Bay marae and the market inquiry's "local hapū" were already there; confirm they stay generic and respectful.
7. **Terms with only a general Te Aka source** (no curriculum citation), especially *whakahohoro* (acceleration), *matūriki* (particle), *wē* (liquid), *kanoirite* (isotope), *orokati tārua* (alliteration) and *kupu ororite* (onomatopoeia). A Māori-medium science, maths or English teacher could confirm the terms kura actually use.
